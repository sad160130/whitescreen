What does burgundy do to your mood?

Color researchers have found that darker reds like burgundy trigger a distinct set of responses compared to their brighter counterparts. While vibrant red activates urgency and energy, burgundy activates something more complex.

Studies on color perception show that burgundy consistently ranks high for associations with:

  • Power and ambition: Executives and leaders often gravitate toward burgundy accessories
  • Sophistication: The color appears frequently in luxury brand palettes
  • Wealth: Deep, saturated colors historically signaled the expense of quality dyes
  • Passion with restraint: The romantic intensity of red, matured and refined
  • Maturity: Burgundy reads as experienced, not juvenile

The Designer's Secret

When luxury brands want to signal "premium but approachable," they often reach for burgundy. It's warm enough to feel inviting but dark enough to maintain exclusivity. That same principle applies when you use a full burgundy screen as a backdrop—instant elevation.

What is the hex code for burgundy?

This particular burgundy balances depth with visibility. Here are the technical details:

  • Hex: #800020
  • RGB: 128, 0, 32
  • HSL: 345°, 100%, 25%
  • Color Family: Dark red-brown with purple undertones

The 345° hue places burgundy firmly in the red family but with that slight purple shift that distinguishes it from pure reds. At 25% lightness, it's dark enough to feel substantial while still reading clearly as a distinct color.

When to Use Burgundy vs. Other Dark Reds

The dark red family has several members. Here's how to choose:

Burgundy vs. Crimson: Crimson is significantly brighter and more vibrant. Use crimson when you need energy and impact; use burgundy when you need sophistication and depth.

Burgundy vs. Maroon: Very similar colors, but burgundy has more purple undertone while maroon leans more toward pure dark red. Burgundy feels more refined; maroon feels more earthy.

Burgundy vs. Red: Red is dramatic and attention-grabbing. Burgundy is dramatic and understated. Red for urgency, burgundy for importance.

Burgundy vs. Rose: Completely different energy. Rose is light and romantic; burgundy is deep and passionate. Rose for softness, burgundy for intensity.

Choose burgundy specifically when you want viewers to feel they're experiencing something premium, mature, or exclusive.
