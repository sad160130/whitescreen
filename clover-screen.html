What is a clover screen?

A clover screen is a full-display tool that fills your monitor with deep, earthy clover green (#384A22)—a dark, grounded hue named after the lucky plant that carpets meadows and forest floors. Clover is nature's whisper in the dark—quieter than emerald, deeper than fern, richer than olive.

This understated green sits low in both brightness and saturation, drawing its character from the shadowy undersides of dense vegetation. Named after the humble three-leafed plant long associated with luck, abundance, and renewal, clover green carries a sense of rootedness that few colors can replicate. It is the color of soil meeting stem, of twilight settling over a summer meadow.

What does clover green do to your mood?

Clover green occupies a deeply calming psychological space—it soothes and steadies without putting you to sleep, grounding your attention the way standing barefoot on earth settles a racing mind. Its darkness adds gravitas while its green warmth maintains a living, organic quality that pure neutrals cannot offer.

  • Growth and renewal — Connects to the persistent vitality of living plants
  • Luck and abundance — Named after a plant symbolizing fortune across cultures
  • Security and shelter — Evokes the protective canopy of dense foliage
  • Quiet confidence — Commands respect through subtlety rather than volume

Why Clover Green Promotes Focus

Research in color psychology suggests that dark, muted greens like clover reduce cortisol levels and lower heart rate, creating an environment conducive to sustained concentration. Unlike brighter greens that energize, clover's low-light quality mimics the calming effect of being beneath a forest canopy, helping the mind settle into productive, meditative flow states.

The Plant Behind the Color

Clover (Trifolium) has been a symbol of luck and protection since ancient Celtic traditions, where druids believed it could ward off evil spirits. The rare four-leaf clover occurs roughly once in every 5,000 plants, making it one of the most famous symbols of good fortune. The plant's rich, dark green leaves inspired this color name—a shade that captures the essence of clover growing thick in shaded meadows.

How is clover different from other dark greens?

Clover occupies a unique niche among dark greens—warmer and more yellow-toned than most, with a muted earthiness that sets it apart from brighter or cooler relatives. Here's how it compares to help you choose the right shade:

Clover vs. Forest Green

Clover (#384A22): Darker, warmer, with yellow-green undertones and an earthy, muted quality.

Forest Green (#228B22): Brighter, more saturated, a bolder pure green. View forest green screen.

Clover vs. Pine

Clover (#384A22): Warmer, yellow-leaning, softer and more organic in feel.

Pine (#01796F): Cooler, teal-leaning, with blue-green undertones and a sharper edge. View pine screen.

Clover vs. Olive

Clover (#384A22): Darker, greener, with more depth and shadow.

Olive (#808000): Lighter, more yellow, with a khaki-like warmth and military association. View olive screen.

Clover vs. Fern

Clover (#384A22): Deeper, more muted, with stronger earthy darkness.

Fern (#4F7942): Lighter, livelier, a brighter mid-tone green with more visible vitality. View fern screen.

Clover vs. Emerald

Clover (#384A22): Much darker, warmer, subdued and grounded in earth tones.

Emerald (#50C878): Bright, jewel-toned, luminous and vivid with a cool sparkle. View emerald screen.
