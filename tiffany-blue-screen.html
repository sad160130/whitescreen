The Robin's Egg Connection

Tiffany Blue was inspired by the color of robin's eggs, which in Victorian times symbolized good luck, happiness, and new beginnings. This association with positive life events—combined with Tiffany & Co.'s exclusive use since 1837—created the modern luxury connotation. The color now triggers anticipation and delight before the box is even opened.

Brand Heritage and Cultural Significance

Few colors in history have become as closely tied to a single brand as Tiffany Blue. Understanding this heritage reveals why the color carries such powerful associations.

A Color With History

Charles Lewis Tiffany selected this distinctive blue-green for the cover of Tiffany's Blue Book in 1837. The choice wasn't random—turquoise and robin's egg blue were immensely popular in Victorian jewelry, and the color of turquoise stones symbolized wealth and protection.

What made Tiffany Blue iconic wasn't just the color itself, but the strict rules around it: Tiffany boxes were never sold, only given with purchases. This scarcity transformed a color into a symbol of gifted luxury.

Modern Influence

Today, Tiffany Blue influences design far beyond jewelry:

  • Wedding industry — One of the most requested colors for elegant ceremonies
  • Luxury packaging — Premium brands use similar shades to signal quality
  • Interior design — Popular for sophisticated, calming spaces
  • Digital branding — Tech companies use it for premium product tiers
