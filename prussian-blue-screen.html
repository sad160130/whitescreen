What is a Prussian blue screen?

A Prussian blue screen is a full-display tool that fills your entire monitor with deep, historic Prussian blue (#003153)—the first modern synthetic pigment ever created. Prussian blue is the color of ink at the bottom of a well—profound, dense, and endlessly deep.

Accidentally discovered in Berlin around 1706 by colormaker Johann Jacob Diesbach, Prussian blue revolutionized the art world. Before its creation, deep blue pigments like ultramarine were more expensive than gold. Prussian blue democratized the color, making it available to artists, textile makers, and eventually to the blueprint process that gave architects their iconic blue drawings.

Why Prussian Blue Works for Focus

Very dark blues like Prussian blue reduce visual stimulation to a minimum, allowing the mind to turn inward. Unlike black, which can feel void-like, Prussian blue retains enough chromatic information to feel alive and engaged. This balance makes it particularly effective for late-night work sessions, writing, and contemplative tasks.

The First Synthetic Pigment

Prussian blue (iron ferrocyanide) was discovered accidentally in 1706 when Diesbach was trying to create a red pigment. A contaminated batch of potash produced an unexpected deep blue instead. It became so important that its chemical study contributed to the founding of modern chemistry.
