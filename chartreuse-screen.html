What is a chartreuse screen?

Why Chartreuse Is So Visible

Chartreuse's exceptional visibility comes from biology. The human eye has peak sensitivity around 555 nanometers—right in the yellow-green range. Studies by the American Optometric Association found that chartreuse yellow-green is more visible than pure yellow, especially in peripheral vision. This is why modern fire trucks, safety vests, and emergency equipment increasingly use chartreuse instead of traditional red or orange.

The Origin of Chartreuse: From Monks to Modernity

Chartreuse has one of the most interesting origin stories of any color name—it comes from an alcoholic beverage crafted by French monks.

The Carthusian Connection

In 1605, the Carthusian monks at the Grande Chartreuse monastery in France received a manuscript containing a recipe for an "Elixir of Long Life." By 1737, they had perfected a green liqueur using 130 herbs, plants, and flowers. This distinctive yellow-green spirit became known as Chartreuse, and by the late 19th century, the color itself took on the liqueur's name.

A Color That Named Itself

Unlike most colors named after natural objects (orange, violet, salmon), chartreuse is one of the few named after a manufactured product. The liqueur's unique hue was so distinctive that when people saw something of similar color, they simply called it "chartreuse."

The monks still produce Chartreuse liqueur today at their monastery in Voiron, France, making it one of the longest continuously produced spirits in the world—and the source of one of our most vibrant color names.
